## Ikigai: The Art of Staying Young While Growing Old

---

### Background

+ **Etymology**
	+ **"Iki" (生き):** Means "life" or "to live."
	+ **"Gai" (甲斐):** Refers to "value," "worth," or "effect."
	+ Together, **ikigai** translates roughly to "the value of living" or "a reason for living."
+ Ikigai is not necessarily tied to grand ambitions or material success. Instead, it often focuses on:
	+ **Simple Pleasures:** Finding joy in daily activities, like savoring a cup of tea, gardening, or connecting with loved ones.
	+ **Community and Relationships:** Many people derive their ikigai from their roles within their families, workplaces, or communities.
	+ **Personal Growth:** Ikigai can also relate to self-improvement and the pursuit of hobbies or interests that bring fulfillment.

---

### **Cultural Context**

+ The concept originates from **Okinawa**, a Japanese island known for its high concentration of centenarians (***blue zone***. 
	+ Residents often attribute their longevity to having a strong sense of ikigai
+ Ikigai is viewed as a deeply embedded aspect of everyday life
	+ Not something one must seek far away but something to cultivate in ordinary moments.

### Ikigai and Logotherapy

+ Victor Frankl: *Man's Search for Meaning*
	+ “Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms—to choose one’s attitude in any given set of circumstances, to choose one’s own way.”
+ In German concentration camps, as in those that would later be built in Japan and Korea, psychiatrists confirmed that the prisoners with the greatest chance of survival were those who had things they wanted to accomplish outside the camp, those who felt a strong need to get out of there alive. 
+ This was true of Frankl, who, after being released and successfully developing the school of logotherapy, realized he had been the first patient of his own practice.

### **Modern Interpretation**

- The Westernized version of ikigai often presents it as the intersection of four domains: 
	- what you **love**
	- what you are **good at** 
	- what the **world needs**
	- what you **can be paid for**
- While this Venn diagram framework can be helpful for career and life planning, it oversimplifies and somewhat commercializes the more subtle and holistic Japanese understanding of ikigai.
	- Highly manualized, but still has its place if treated as an evolving framework

## Goal Hierarchy of Personal Strivings

---


+ **Goal Hierarchy of Personal Strivings:** organization of an individual’s goals or pursuits into a structured framework
+ **Top-Level Goals (Core Values or Life Purposes):** most abstract and overarching goals, often tied to one’s identity, values, and sense of meaning.
+ **Mid-Level Goals (Personal Strivings or Projects):** specific, ongoing goals or pursuits that align with top-level goals.
	 - Bridge between abstract aspirations and concrete actions.
     - Often involve long-term commitment and recurring efforts.
 + **Lower-Level Goals (Concrete Tasks or Actions):** specific, actionable steps taken to accomplish mid-level goals.in."
   - **Characteristics:**
     - Short-term and measurable.
     - Directly tied to day-to-day behavior.
