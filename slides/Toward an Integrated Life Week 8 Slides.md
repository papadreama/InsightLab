## Meditation: Simple anchor practice
- Periodically, we'll drop in the question, "What is the attitude in my mind right now?"
- See if you can notice the *attitude/feeling/sentiment toward what's arising*, rather than just what's arising - shift from *contents* to *context*
	- For example, you might notice you're tired. Can you also notice how you feel about it? Annoyed? Dismissive? Accepting? Relieved?
	- Is there resistance to the practice? If so, what's that like? 
		- Can you bring some curiosity beyond the surface reactions that come up? 
- Toward the last minute, we'll let go of the anchor and try a simple cue: "be still and know..."
	- ...that you are sitting, ...that you are breathing, ...that you are hearing, ...that you are feeling, ...that you are

---

### Discussion

1. Were there any specific thoughts or sensations that stood out to you during the meditation?
2. Did you notice any resistance or distractions during the meditation? How did you respond to them?
3. Did you feel a sense of connection to yourself or to the present moment?
4. Were there any moments of stillness or clarity that surprised you? What did they feel like?
5. Did you notice any patterns or recurring thoughts that arose during your meditation?
6. How did your body feel during and after the meditation?

## Inner Freedom

"Just as the great oceans have but one taste, the taste of salt, so too there is but one taste fundamental to all true teachings, and this is the taste of freedom."

---

### Inner Freedom + Mindfulness

+ The three "poisons:"
	+ **Greed/Attachment**
		+ Stuck in a cycle of dissatisfaction
		+ Freedom from greed: generosity + non-attachment
	+ **Hatred/Aversion** 
		+ Reactivity, anger, pushing away
		+ Freedom from hatred: compassion + lovingkindness
	+ **Delusion**
		+ Seeing oneself as a separate and fixed entity
		+ Freedom from delusion: wisdom + mindfulness
+ Equanimity
+ Presence

---

### Inner Freedom and Creativity/Self-Expression

+ **Freedom from perfectionism**: Allowing oneself to create and act without attachment to ideals
+ **Vulnerability**: Brené Brown defines vulnerability as the willingness to embrace uncertainty, risk, and emotional exposure.
	+ Not a weakness, but as courageous in the willingness to take off our armor
	+ Open to authentic connection with others (and ourselves)
+ **Exploration and Play:** Kids explore with reckless abandon. We tend to lose that playful exploratory spirit as age, as we lose touch with beginner's mind

## Resistance

+ This is an idea author Steven Pressfield explores in his book, *The War of Art* (the title is a play on the Chinese classic, *The Art of War* by Sun Tzu)
+ Resistance refers to the force that prevents us from pursuing our creative endeavors and reaching our full potential
+ It's not just for artists/writers
	+ Living is an art
	+ We're all creating meaning, solving problems, generating ideas

---

### The Nature of Resistance

+ Universal, impersonal force acting against our creative energies
	+ Universal in its existence, but not necessarily in the form it takes
	+ We all experience it in different ways
+ Like inertia - tendency of an object at rest to stay at rest
	+ Maintains the status quo

---

### Manifestations of Resistance

- Procrastination
- Fear and anxiety
- Self-doubt
- Rationalization
- Distraction
- Inner criticism
- Addiction

---

### The Importance of Battling Resistance

+ The poet Mary Oliver claimed, "The most regretful people on earth are those who felt the call to creative work, who felt their own creative power restive and uprising, and gave to it neither power nor time." 
+ Not about people who never met their creative potential because of *external factors,* but who let their own internal resistance take hold. 
+ Pressfield believes that by recognizing and confronting resistance, we can unlock our creative potential and live more fulfilling lives - think, eudaimonia.

---

## Overcoming Resistance

Overcoming resistance is necessary for personal and creative growth. Here are a few of Pressfield's suggestions:

- Recognize resistance as a separate, impersonal force - not oneself.
- Transition from an amateur to a professional mindset.
	- "Inspiration is for amateurs. The rest of us just show up and get to work...All the best ideas come out of the process; they come out of the work itself." - Chuck Close
- Develop a daily practice and routine.
- Embrace the challenges and "misery" that sometimes (maybe inevitably) come with creative work.
- Show up consistently, regardless of self-doubt.

---

### Practical Techniques

1. Use the "two-minute rule": If a task takes less than two minutes, do it immediately to build momentum.
2. Implement the "Pomodoro Technique": Work in focused 25-minute intervals followed by short breaks.
3. Practice "Morning Pages": Start your day with three pages of stream-of-consciousness writing to clear your mind.
+ Overcoming resistance is an ongoing process. 
+ As Pressfield puts it, 
	+ "We can never eliminate Resistance. It will never go away. But we can outsmart it, and we can enlist allies that are as powerful as it is".
