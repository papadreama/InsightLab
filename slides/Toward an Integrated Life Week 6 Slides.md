## The Four Sublime States

+ **Loving-Kindness (Mettā):** Loving-kindness is the wish for all beings to be happy and to have the causes of happiness.
+ **Compassion (Karuṇā):** Compassion is the wish for all beings to be free from suffering and its causes. 
+ **Sympathetic Joy (Muditā):** Sympathetic joy, or empathetic joy, is the ability to rejoice in the happiness and success of others.
+ **Equanimity (Upekkhā):** Equanimity is an even-minded mental state or dispositional tendency toward all experiences, people, and objects, regardless of their origin or their affective valence (pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral).

---

## Near and Far Enemies

+ **Loving-Kindness (Mettā)**
	+ **Near enemy:** Attachment or selfish affection
	+ **Far enemies:** Hatred, ill-will, aversion
+ **Compassion (Karuṇā)**
	+ **Near enemy:** Pity or grief based on worldly life
	+ **Far enemy:** Cruelty
+ **Sympathetic Joy (Muditā)**
	+ **Near enemy:** Frivolity or superficial joy
	+ **Far enemy:** Envy, jealousy, resentment
+ **Equanimity (Upekkhā)**
	+ **Near enemy:** Indifference or apathy
	+ **Far enemies:** Attachment and aversion (or anger)

---

### Discussion

+ The near enemies as especially difficult to spot, because, from the outside, they can resemble the virtuous state. 
+ Take equanimity, for example. 
	+ A person who is genuinely indifferent toward others and their well-being, might seem, calm, cool, and collected. 
+ Consider your list of core values. 
	+ What are the near and far enemies of your core values?

### Practice: Caring Moment + Lovingkindness


+ What makes this a mindfulness practice is the orientation of curiosity we bring to it, without any expectations. 
+ As with every other practice we've done, we can investigate what happens in the mind and body as we repeat these phrases. 
	 + If there's resistance, notice that. What's that feel like?
	+ If there's a sense of openness, notice that. What's it feel like?
	+ If there's judgment, notice that. What's that feel like?
	+ If there's happiness, notice that. What's that feel like?
	+ If there's sadness, notice that. What's that feel like?


---

#### Basic Instructions

+ After a short period of settling, we'll start with a caring moment practice
	+ Visualize a caring moment: interaction with a person, a place, a moment with a pet, even a song
	+ Replay the moment as your anchor and notice what comes up
+ Move into loving-kindness
	+ May you be happy. 
	+ May you be healthy.  
	+ May you live with ease. 
+ Start with self, then a loved one, then a neutral person, then a difficult person, then everyone, everywhere, all at once

## Golden Mean

---

### Aristotle's Ethics

+ The highest good: Eudaemonia
	+ 1. It must be desirable for itself - it's an end unto itself.
	+ 2. It isn't desirable for the sake of some other good - it isn't a means to some other end.
	+ 3. Whatever else we consider *good* must be good is necessarily desirable for the sake of that highest good.
+ What is the (unique) *ergon* of the human being?
	+ Ergon: function, task, work


---

### The Golden Mean

+ The Golden Mean helps define these virtues by positioning them between extremes:
	+ Virtues are habits of choosing the mean between extremes
	+ The mean is relative to the individual and the situation, not a fixed point
	+ Finding the mean requires practical wisdom (phronesis) to judge the right action in each context

---

### Examples


+ 1. Courage
   - Deficiency: Cowardice
   - Mean: Courage
   - Excess: Recklessness
+ 2. Temperance
   - Deficiency: Insensibility
   - Mean: Temperance
   - Excess: Self-indulgence
+ 3. Generosity
   - Deficiency: Stinginess
   - Mean: Generosity
   - Excess: Wastefulness

--

+ 4. Truthfulness
   - Deficiency: Self-deprecation
   - Mean: Truthfulness
   - Excess: Boastfulness
+ 5. Wit
   - Deficiency: Boorishness
   - Mean: Wittiness
   - Excess: Buffoonery
+ 6. Modesty
   - Deficiency: Shamelessness
   - Mean: Modesty
   - Excess: Bashfulness

## Reason or Emotion

+ What are the roles of reason and emotion in the development of values/virtues?
+ Aristotle: reason is the unique capacity of the human being
	+ It's proper application is the source of our wellbeing/flourishing
	+ Stoicism
+ Martha Nusbaum: **Emotions as Appraisals**
	+ Modern critique
	+ Emotions are appraisals that attribute significant value to external objects or persons, which can affect an individual's flourishing
